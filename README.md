## How This Repository Is Organized

I wanted the repository structure to follow the journey of the batch without letting the structure become the focus of the journey.

Each numbered directory represents a section of the batch and contains the work related to that stage of my learning. As I move through the batch, these directories will gradually become a record of what I learned and implemented at each point.

The `live-sessions` directory is kept separately for discussions that happen during the occasional LIVE sessions. These may include doubts, announcements, feedback, project-related discussions, or decisions made during the batch.

The `README.md` acts as the map of the journey, while the directories contain the work and learning behind it.

## How I Document Each Section

I don't want the documentation for each section to become a copy of the instructor's notes or a list of topics I went through.

Instead, I want each section to reflect what the batch actually became for me.

Whenever appropriate, I document a section through four perspectives:

- **What I learned** — the concepts, tools, and techniques I picked up from the section.
- **My understanding** — how I understand those concepts in my own words and how I see them fitting together.
- **Experiments** — things I tried beyond simply following the batch examples, including small tests and experiments that helped me understand how something actually behaves.
- **Further exploration** — ideas, questions, or related topics that I want to investigate beyond what was covered directly in the section.

Not every section has to contain the same amount of material. Some concepts may need detailed explanations and experiments, while others may simply need a concise record of what I learned.

The purpose is not to make every section look complete. The purpose is to make the repository reflect how my understanding develops over time.

One of the most interesting parts of this journey is **VeoLMS**.

The Vision for Open Education Learning Management System. It is an actual, evolving, open-source project being built in public. While it is now part of the learning material through **Building VeoLMS: Idea to Production**, the project itself has its own life beyond the regular batch progression.
</details>
<details>
<summary><strong>Joining the journey late</strong></summary>

I joined the Node.js batch on 17 August 2026, well after the batch had already started a year earlier. By the time I joined, the development of VeoLMS was already underway and it's frontend was already [live](https://veolms.org/).

**GitHub:** [https://github.com/veolms](https://github.com/veolms)  
**VeoLMS Project:** [https://veolms.org/](https://veolms.org/)  
**VeoLMS Platform/Application:** [https://dev.veolms.org/](https://dev.veolms.org/)  
However, being late does not mean I have to miss the story behind it.
</details>
<details>
<summary><strong>About the Council</strong></summary>
A prototype-based selection process was conducted to form a six-member council for the project, with Anurag Sir himself being one of the members. The opportunity to submit a problem for the selection process was opened for beyond just the existing students enrolled in Node.js batch, making participation in the project a separate opportunity rather than simply another batch assignment. This council will decide every small descisions in the future for the VeoLMS.

The recordings of the project's online meetings allow me to go back and see discussions that happened before I joined the batch. These meetings bring together the six-member council working on VeoLMS and cover much more than writing code — including user experience, design decisions, individual components, project decisions, feedback, and the many small discussions that gradually shape a real software product. This is particularly interesting to me because these are things that are usually invisible when we only see the finished repository. I can see not only *what* was built, but also some of the thinking, discussion, and decisions made by the council while deciding *how* it should be built.

```The journey toward the project has also involved students beyond the enrollments in the batch.```

I find this part of the journey especially valuable because it connects learning with something much closer to real software development: working with other people, discussing decisions, thinking about users, dealing with constraints, experimenting with designs, and gradually turning an idea into a working product.
</details>
<details>
<summary><strong>Vision behind - Vision for Open Education</strong></summary>
Looking at the batch progression and the way VeoLMS has developed alongside it, I sometimes wonder whether the depth of the curriculum and the project were always meant to grow alongside each other. That is my own interpretation, not something I want to present as an official explanation of the mentor's intentions. Whatever the original vision was, I can see that the result has become much more interesting than simply finishing a backend batch.
</details>
For me, VeoLMS represents a point where the things I learn in the batch can be viewed in the context of a real, evolving system. As I progress, I want to use opportunities like this to connect individual concepts with larger software systems — not only understanding how something works in isolation, but also seeing how those pieces come together when building something real.

That is where my learning can go beyond the curriculum: following the project, understanding its decisions, exploring its code, experimenting with ideas, and eventually contributing where I can.
